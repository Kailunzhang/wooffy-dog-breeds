    How these numbers work

    Each breed’s figures come from its Wooffy first-year cost guide. The calculator adds up the line items for the options you pick: the puppy price or adoption fee, year-one vet care, spay/neuter, 12 months of food and insurance, grooming, and setup. Items a guide doesn’t list are filled in with the typical figure for dogs of the same size and marked “(estimate)”. The region setting moves vet, spay/neuter and grooming prices about 10% down or 15% up, a rough guide in line with US regional price levels from the BEA, not local pricing.     Dog cost FAQ

    How much does a dog cost in the first year?

    It depends most on the breed and the puppy price. At our default settings (suburb, breeder puppy, pet insurance, professional grooming), first-year totals across the 164 breeds and doodle mixes here run from $1,580–$4,010 for the Chihuahua to $5,159–$11,780 for the Tibetan Mastiff. A Great Dane comes to $4,752–$9,220.

    Is adopting a dog cheaper than buying from a breeder?

    Usually. Shelter and rescue fees usually run $50–$500, with puppies at the high end, according to Petfinder. Adoption fees often cover first vaccines, spay/neuter and a microchip, so ask what’s included. The median breeder price range in our guides is $1,200–$2,500.

    How much does pet insurance add to the first year?

    In our cost guides, premiums average $38–$75 a month across breeds, or about $460–$910 a year. Insurance won’t cover conditions that show signs before you enroll or during the waiting period. If you skip it, consider setting money aside for emergencies: a dog ER exam averages about $135 before tests or treatment, and a night in the hospital averages about $619 (CareCredit, 2025).

    How much does a dog cost after the first year?

    Less, because the puppy price, spay/neuter and setup are one-time costs. Across our guides, the median for year two and beyond is $1,490–$2,700 a year.

    Why doesn’t the total match the breed’s cost guide?

    The calculator adds up every line item for the options you choose. A guide’s headline total may be rounded or leave out the puppy price, and items a guide doesn’t list are filled in with the typical figure for dogs of the same size.
